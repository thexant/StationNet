Notice

This manual describes StationNet Version 1.0 as distributed in November 2026. StationNet is under continuing development, and later releases may differ in detail from the descriptions given here.

StationNet is modular. Each station is set up by its own operator, who decides which services are offered, who may use them, and what material they contain. The services, channels, books, games, programs, and worlds you find on a particular station may therefore differ from the examples in this manual.

Screen names, button captions, and commands are printed exactly as they appear on the screen. Where the station operator may change a setting, the manual says so.

Product names mentioned in this manual are used for identification only and may be trademarks of their respective owners.

Preface

StationNet is a self-hosted communications and information service. A single computer on a home, school, club, or office network runs the station, and everyone else reaches it with an ordinary web browser. No outside service, subscription, or Internet connection is required once a station is installed.

This manual is written first for the people who use a station: those who read the bulletins, exchange mail, listen to the radio, read in the library, work in the Office programs, or play in the MUD. Parts I through V assume no knowledge of StationNet beyond basic familiarity with a computer and a web browser.

Parts VI and VII serve two smaller audiences. Part VI is for the station operator, called the SYSOP, who installs StationNet, starts and stops stations, and administers accounts and content. Part VII is a short orientation for developers who wish to write programs, themes, or game worlds for StationNet.

How This Manual Is Organized

Part I, Getting Started, explains what a station is, how to connect, how to create an account, and how the screen is arranged. It ends with a short guided session.

Part II, Communication and Community, covers the user directory, chat, private mail, the bulletin board, the imageboard, events, and the file archive.

Part III, Information and Entertainment, covers radio, television, videos, the library, the reference library (WIKI), programs, the arcade, and the MUD.

Part IV, Office, covers the six Office programs: Documents, Sheets, Presentations, Draw, Agenda, and BASIC.

Part V, Personalization and Everyday Use, collects the settings and habits that apply to every service: appearance, sound, notifications, installation on a phone or desktop, station hours, and account security.

Part VI, Station Hosting and Administration, is for SYSOPs.

Part VII, Developer's Reference, is for programmers.

The Appendices contain a keyboard reference, a quick reference to common operations, a troubleshooting guide, a table of modules and permissions, and a glossary.

Conventions Used in This Manual

MONOSPACED CAPITALS show text that appears on the screen, such as a tab name or button caption: for example, choose COMPOSE +.

Monospaced lower case shows something you type exactly, such as a MUD command (/look) or a file name (station.json).

Words in angle brackets stand for a value you supply. In /go <direction>, replace <direction> with an actual direction, such as north.

Choose means click with the mouse or tap on a touch screen. Press refers to a key on the keyboard.

Three kinds of notices appear in the text:

NOTE

A NOTE gives additional information or a helpful suggestion.

CAUTION

A CAUTION warns of an action that may cause inconvenience or loss of work.

WARNING

A WARNING identifies an action that permanently destroys information or affects other people's access to the station.

Part I

Getting Started

1. Introducing StationNet

1.1 What a Station Is

A station is one running copy of StationNet together with everything it keeps: its name, its member accounts, its messages, and its files. The station runs on a single host computer, which may be a desktop computer, a laptop, a Raspberry Pi, or an Android phone. Other people reach the station over the local network with a web browser.

Every station has a name chosen by its operator and a callsign, a short code assigned when the station is first set up. Every member also receives a callsign. Callsigns appear beside usernames throughout the system, in the form username - [CALLSIGN], so that members can be identified reliably.

One host computer may run several stations at once, each with its own name, network port, accounts, and content. The stations do not share information with one another.

1.2 Services and Modules

The facilities a station offers are called services. Each service is provided by a module that the SYSOP may turn on or off. Bulletin board, announcements, chat, mail, radio, files, the user directory, and programs are normally on. The imageboard, events, WIKI, videos, library, arcade, television, Office, and MUD are off until the SYSOP turns them on.

A service that is turned off does not appear in the navigation bar and cannot be opened. If this manual describes a service you cannot find, the station you are using does not offer it.

The SYSOP also supplies most of a station's content: the radio programming, the television channels, the books and videos, the games, and the MUD worlds. StationNet provides the means of presenting this material; it does not supply the material itself.

1.3 Classes of User

StationNet distinguishes three classes of user.

Guests are visitors who have not signed in. If the station permits guest access, a guest may read public services such as the bulletin board and announcements, but may not post, chat, or send mail.

Members are visitors who have signed in to an account. The status bar at the bottom of the screen shows a member as MEMBER.

SYSOPs are station operators. The person who sets up a station becomes its SYSOP and is shown as SYSOP. A SYSOP has two additional tabs, SYSOP CONTROL and MODERATION, and additional controls throughout the system. A SYSOP may also delegate particular duties to members, such as hosting radio broadcasts, creating events, moderating the imageboard, or building MUD worlds.

Parts I through V describe what a member can do. Operations reserved for SYSOPs or delegated members are identified as such.

1.4 What You Need

To use a station you need:

  • A computer, tablet, or phone on the same network as the station.
  • A current web browser, such as Chrome, Edge, Firefox, or Safari.
  • The station's address, which the SYSOP will give you.
  • An account on the station, unless you intend only to browse as a guest.

Some services require more. Live voice on the radio requires a microphone and, when you connect from a computer other than the host, a secure (https://) address. Installing the station as an application on a phone or desktop also requires a secure address. These requirements are described where they apply.

2. Connecting and Signing In

2.1 Opening the Station

Every station has an address of the form

http://<host-address>:<port>

For example, http://192.168.1.20:8080. The SYSOP will tell you the correct address. If you are sitting at the host computer itself, the address is usually http://127.0.0.1:8080.

Type the address into the address bar of your browser and press Enter. The station's welcome page appears, showing the station's name in large characters, its access policy, and, if the SYSOP has written one, a message about the station.

NOTE

Bookmark the station's address, or install it as an application (see Chapter 30), so that you do not need to type it each time.

2.2 Registration Policies

A strip at the top of the sign-in page reports the station's registration policy:

PUBLIC ENROLLMENT OPEN
Anyone may create an account.
PRIVATE / INVITATION ONLY
A single-use invitation code from the SYSOP is required.
ENROLLMENT CLOSED
New accounts cannot be created. Ask the SYSOP for assistance.

2.3 Creating an Account

To create an account:

  1. On the sign-in page, choose NO ACCOUNT? CREATE ONE ->, or on a private station HAVE AN INVITATION? ENROLL ->.
  2. On a private station, enter the invitation code in the Invitation code field. If you followed an invitation link, the code is already filled in.
  3. Enter a Username of 3 to 24 characters. Letters, numbers, the underscore (_), and the hyphen (-) are allowed.
  4. Enter a Password of at least 10 characters, and enter it again in Confirm password.
  5. Choose CREATE ACCOUNT.

The station creates the account, assigns you a random callsign, signs you in, and displays the confirmation Account linked: <username> - [<callsign>]. Usernames cannot be changed later, so choose carefully.

2.4 Signing In

  1. Choose LOGIN or > AUTHENTICATE.
  2. Enter your Username and Password.
  3. If this is your own device, you may select Remember me on this device.
  4. Choose LOGIN.

When you are signed in, the station displays Link established. Welcome, <username> and opens the STATION page.

With Remember me on this device selected, the station keeps you signed in on that browser across restarts of the browser and of the station until you disconnect. Without it, you are signed out when the browser is closed.

CAUTION

Do not select Remember me on this device on a computer that other people use.

2.5 If You Forget Your Password

StationNet does not use e-mail and cannot send you a reset link. Instead:

  1. Attempt to sign in. After a failed attempt, a FORGOT PASSWORD button appears.
  2. Make sure your username is entered, then choose FORGOT PASSWORD.
  3. The station generates a temporary password and sends it privately to the station's SYSOPs. Contact a SYSOP, who will give it to you.
  4. Sign in with the temporary password, then change it at once (Section 4.4).

2.6 Disconnecting

To sign out, open the STATION tab and choose [ DISCONNECT ] in the CURRENT OPERATOR panel. The station displays Connection closed. Disconnecting also ends any direct chats you have open and turns off your radio.

3. The Station Workspace

3.1 The Screen

Every station page has the same general arrangement.

The masthead. The station's name, drawn in large text characters, appears at the top. A short line of text, the tagline, may appear beneath it. The SYSOP sets both.

The station line. Beneath the masthead, a line shows YOUR STATION, the SYSTEM DATE/TIME, and several switches: SFX, which turns interface sounds on or off; VFX, which turns a screen scan-line effect on or off; and, where the station has a theme tune, MUSIC. These switches are described in Chapter 28. If the station keeps regular hours, a second line shows them (Chapter 31).

The navigation bar. A row of tab names, separated by asterisks, leads to each service: STATION, BULLETIN BOARD, ANNOUNCEMENTS, CHAT, MAIL, RADIO, FILES, USERS, PROGRAMS, and others the station offers. The tab for the page you are on is highlighted. CHAT and MAIL show a count when something new is waiting.

The work area. The service you have chosen occupies the middle of the screen. Messages from the station appear above it, prefixed SYS://.

The radio player. When the radio is available, a small player is docked at the edge of every page. See Chapter 13.

The status bar. The bottom line shows the station callsign, your username and callsign, and your class (MEMBER or SYSOP). It also contains the link GET YOUR OWN STATION, which leads to a page of StationNet installation files if the SYSOP has provided them.

3.2 The STATION Page

The STATION tab opens the Service Directory. On the left is a numbered list of the station's services, each marked * ONLINE. Choose any entry to open that service.

On the right, the CURRENT OPERATOR panel shows your username and class and offers [ PROFILE ] and [ DISCONNECT ]. Beneath it, the STATION ACTIVITY panel summarizes recent events: the newest bulletin and reply, the latest chat message, the newest file and member, your unread mail, radio broadcasts now on the air, and current television listings. Each item is a link to the service concerned.

3.3 Conventions Common to All Services

StationNet services share a small number of conventions. Once learned, they apply everywhere.

Buttons are shown in capitals. A button ending in +, such as NEW BULLETIN +, creates something new.

Item menus. Items you are allowed to change, such as your own posts, carry a small downward triangle (▾). Choose it to reveal actions such as DELETE. StationNet asks you to confirm before anything is removed.

Reporting. Items written by other people carry a small ▾ actions menu. Open it and choose REPORT, then type a reason of 10 to 500 characters when asked to send that item to the SYSOPs for review. You may have one open report on any item and may file up to ten reports a day.

Times are displayed in your own device's time zone.

Keyboard navigation. When you are not typing in a field, pressing / moves the keyboard focus to the navigation bar (or, in the MUD, to the command line). Press Esc to release it. Individual services add their own keys; these are listed in Appendix A.

Guests. A guest who chooses a link that requires an account is sent to the sign-in page and, after signing in, is returned to the page requested.

4. Your Profile and Account

4.1 Viewing Your Profile

Your profile is the page other members see when they look you up. To open it, choose [ PROFILE ] on the STATION page, or choose your name in the user directory.

A profile shows whether the member is online, the time last active, the callsign, a short Status line, birthday and gender if supplied, the date the member joined, and an About section.

4.2 Editing Your Profile

  1. Open your profile and choose [ EDIT PROFILE ]. (The USERS tab also has an EDIT MY PROFILE button.)
  2. Complete any of the following fields. All are optional.
    • Profile photo: a PNG, JPEG, GIF, or WebP image. It is reduced to fit within 128 by 128 pixels and replaces any earlier photo.
    • Status: a line of up to 120 characters shown under your name.
    • About me: up to 2,000 characters.
    • Birthday: MM/DD, or YYYY-MM-DD to include the year.
    • Gender: up to 40 characters.
  3. Choose UPDATE MY PROFILE.

4.3 Theme Preferences

If the SYSOP permits personal themes, the profile editing page also contains Theme preferences, where you may choose your own colours. See Section 28.1.

4.4 Changing Your Password

  1. Open your profile and choose [ EDIT PROFILE ].
  2. Under Change password, enter your Existing password.
  3. Enter a New password of at least 10 characters and confirm it.
  4. Choose CHANGE PASSWORD.

Your StationNet password is separate from the Office password described in Chapter 21.

4.5 Deleting Your Account

The Delete account section at the foot of the profile editing page permanently removes your account and everything you have contributed: files, videos, images, Office documents, bulletins, feed posts and comments, chat messages, mail, events, radio uploads, saved program data, and MUD characters. Content in threads you started may also be removed.

To proceed, choose DELETE ACCOUNT and type your username. Choose CONFIRM, select the final CONFIRM box, and choose DELETE.

WARNING

Account deletion takes effect immediately and cannot be undone. The SYSOP cannot restore a deleted account.

The account that set up the station cannot be deleted in this way.

5. A First Session

This short session introduces the services most members use daily. It assumes you have an account and that the station offers the usual services.

  1. Sign in. Open the station's address, choose LOGIN, and sign in as described in Section 2.4. The STATION page appears.
  2. Look around. Read the STATION ACTIVITY panel to see what has happened recently. Choose ANNOUNCEMENTS to read any notices from the SYSOP.
  3. Complete your profile. Choose [ PROFILE ], then [ EDIT PROFILE ]. Write a short Status line, such as New here - say hello, and choose UPDATE MY PROFILE.
  4. Read the bulletin board. Choose BULLETIN BOARD. Choose any subject to read it and its replies. To answer, type in Post reply and choose SEND REPLY.
  5. Say hello in chat. Choose CHAT, type a greeting in the box at the bottom, and press Enter.
  6. Send a letter. Choose USERS, find another member, and choose MAIL. Enter a subject and message and choose DELIVER PRIVATE MAIL.
  7. Turn on the radio. In the radio player at the edge of the screen, choose POWER: OFF to switch it on, then choose SCAN > to find a station that is on the air.
  8. Disconnect. When you have finished, open STATION and choose [ DISCONNECT ].
Part II

Communication and Community

6. Users and Profiles

6.1 The User Directory

The USERS tab lists every registered member. Each entry shows the member's photo, ONLINE or OFFLINE, username, callsign, status line, and the time last active. SYSOPs are marked SYSOP.

To change the order, choose an option under SORT BY: Most recently active, Newest registered, Oldest registered, or Alphabetical (A–Z). Then choose SORT. Long lists are divided into pages; use PREVIOUS, NEXT, or a page number.

Each entry offers PROFILE and MAIL. Members who are online also offer DIRECT CHAT.

6.2 Contacting Another Member

From another member's profile you may:

  • Choose OPEN DIRECT CHAT, if the member is online (Section 7.2).
  • Choose SEND PRIVATE MAIL (Chapter 8).
  • Report the member's behaviour with REPORT USER in the profile's ▾ menu (Section 3.3).

6.3 User Feeds

Where the station offers user feeds, each profile has a feed of short posts beneath the profile details.

To post to your own feed, open your profile, type up to 200 characters in Share a thought, and choose POST TO YOUR FEED.

To react to a post, choose one of the five reaction buttons beneath it (✓, XD, <3, ;_;, >:c). You may hold one reaction per post; choosing another replaces it.

To comment, choose COMMENT beneath the post, type up to 500 characters, and choose COMMENT again. When you comment on another member's post, that member is notified by private mail. A profile shows the three newest comments on each post; choose VIEW ALL COMMENTS to see the rest.

You may delete your own posts and comments from their ▾ menus. The station limits the rate of posting: five posts in ten minutes, twenty comments an hour, and sixty reaction changes an hour.

7. Chat

7.1 Station Chat

Station chat is a single public conversation open to every member. Its messages are kept, so you can read what was said before you arrived.

  1. Choose CHAT. The most recent messages are shown, and new ones appear automatically.
  2. Type your message, up to 1,000 characters, in the box labelled Transmit as.
  3. Press Enter, or choose SEND. Press Shift+Enter to begin a new line without sending.

To report a public message, use the report control shown on the message.

7.2 Direct Chat

Direct chat is a private, temporary conversation between two members who are both online.

To start a direct chat, choose DIRECT CHAT beside the member's name in the user directory, or OPEN DIRECT CHAT on their profile. Type and send messages as in station chat.

When someone writes to you, a count appears on the CHAT tab and an alert naming the sender appears near the top of every page. Choose the sender's name to open the conversation.

NOTE

Direct chat is not stored. The conversation is erased when either member disconnects, and it cannot be recovered later. Use private mail for anything you wish to keep.

If a SYSOP has muted your chat, the message Your chat is muted appears and messages cannot be sent until the restriction ends.

8. Private Mail

Private mail is a permanent, private message service between members of the station.

8.1 Reading Mail

Choose MAIL. The Inbox lists messages sent to you, with unread messages highlighted. A count on the MAIL tab shows how many messages are unread. Choose SENT MAIL to list messages you have sent.

Choose a message to open it. Messages between you and another member are shown together as one conversation, oldest first.

8.2 Sending Mail

  1. Choose COMPOSE +. (Or choose MAIL beside a member's name.)
  2. Select the Recipient from the list of registered members.
  3. Enter a Subject of up to 120 characters.
  4. Type the Message, up to 10,000 characters.
  5. Choose DELIVER PRIVATE MAIL.

8.3 Replying and Deleting

To reply, open the conversation, type in the reply box at the bottom, and choose SEND REPLY. To remove a message from your own mailbox, choose DELETE beside it. Deleting a message does not remove it from the other member's mailbox.

The station also uses private mail for some notices: feed comments, SYSOP warnings, and password reset messages sent to SYSOPs.

9. Bulletin Board, Announcements, and Polls

9.1 The Bulletin Board

The bulletin board holds public discussions, called bulletins, each with its own replies. Choose BULLETIN BOARD to see the list. Each row gives the author, the subject with an excerpt, and the number of replies. A POLL tag marks a bulletin that includes a poll.

To read a bulletin, choose its subject.

To reply, type in Post reply at the foot of the bulletin and choose SEND REPLY.

To post a new bulletin:

  1. Choose NEW BULLETIN +.
  2. Enter a Subject (3 to 120 characters) and a Message (up to 5,000 characters).
  3. Optionally, open ADVANCED to add a poll or set an automatic removal date (Sections 9.3 and 9.4).
  4. Choose POST.

You may delete your own bulletins and replies from their ▾ menus. Deleting a bulletin also deletes all of its replies.

9.2 Announcements

The ANNOUNCEMENTS tab carries official notices from the SYSOP. Only SYSOPs may post announcements; everyone, including guests, may read them. An announcement may include a poll.

9.3 Polls

A poll is a question with up to ten responses, attached to a bulletin or announcement when it is first published.

To vote, select a response (or several, if the poll is marked MULTIPLE VOTES ALLOWED) and choose VOTE. Results are shown after you have voted. You may later choose CHANGE VOTE or CLEAR VOTE.

To add a poll when posting, open ADVANCED, select Add a poll, enter the Question and responses, and if desired select Allow multiple votes. The author of a poll may end voting with CLOSE POLL.

9.4 Automatic Removal

Under ADVANCED, the AUTO-REMOVAL fields let you choose a date and time at which a bulletin will be removed automatically. The time zone defaults to your device's own. Leave the date and time empty to keep the bulletin indefinitely. Bulletins with a removal date show AUTO-REMOVES and the time.

10. Imageboard

The imageboard is a single station-wide board of picture threads. It is available only to signed-in members.

10.1 Browsing

Choose IMAGEBOARD to see the Thread catalog. Each thread shows a preview image, subject, author, number of replies, and the time it was last bumped (that is, last replied to). Pinned threads appear first. Choose a thread's picture or subject to open it, and choose any picture in a thread to see it at full size.

10.2 Starting a Thread

  1. Choose NEW THREAD +.
  2. Enter a Subject. A Message is optional.
  3. Choose an Image. PNG, JPEG, GIF, and WebP files of up to 10 MB are accepted. Animated images are shown by their first frame.
  4. Choose POST THREAD.

10.3 Replying

Beneath the thread, type a Message, attach an Image, or both, and choose POST REPLY. A thread marked LOCKED accepts no replies.

10.4 Limits

The SYSOP sets the number of threads the board keeps. When a new thread would exceed the limit, the oldest threads are removed with their pictures. The SYSOP may also limit the number of pictures in each thread; after the limit is reached, text replies are still accepted.

SYSOPs, and members granted imageboard moderation, may pin, lock, and delete threads.

11. Events

The events calendar announces station activities and lets members say whether they will attend.

11.1 Viewing Events

Choose EVENTS. Upcoming events and events in progress are listed first, followed by past and cancelled events. Choose an event to see its description, start and end times in your own time zone, location, link, a countdown, and the members who are going or interested.

11.2 Responding

On the event page, choose GOING or INTERESTED. The chosen button is marked with a tick. To change your mind, choose the other button, or WITHDRAW.

11.3 Creating an Event

The SYSOP decides who may create events: SYSOPs only, members of selected groups, or all members. If you are permitted, CREATE EVENT + appears on the EVENTS page.

  1. Choose CREATE EVENT +.
  2. Enter a Name and Description.
  3. Enter the Start date and Start time, and optionally the end date and time.
  4. Check the Timezone. It defaults to your device's time zone and is given in the standard form Region/City, for example America/Edmonton.
  5. Optionally enter a Location and a URL.
  6. Choose CREATE EVENT.

The organizer may later choose EDIT or CANCEL EVENT. A cancelled event remains listed so that members know it will not take place.

12. File Archive

The file archive is the station's shared collection of downloadable files. Choose FILES to open it.

12.1 Finding and Downloading Files

The archive is divided into five categories: General, Documents, Images, Software, and Other. Choose ALL or a category name to filter the list. Each entry shows the category, size, name, description, uploader, and date. Picture files show a small preview.

Choose DOWNLOAD to save a file to your device.

12.2 Uploading Files

  1. Choose UPLOAD FILE +.
  2. Choose the File, select a Category, and add a Description of up to 500 characters.
  3. Choose UPLOAD TO ARCHIVE.

The heading of the archive shows how much of the station's total storage is in use, and the upload page shows the largest file accepted. The SYSOP may limit uploading to SYSOPs or turn it off entirely, in which case UPLOAD FILE + does not appear.

You may delete files you uploaded from their ▾ menus.

CAUTION

Files in the archive are visible to every member. Do not upload private documents or material you do not have the right to share.

Part III

Information and Entertainment

13. Radio

The StationNet radio is a shared receiver and transmitter. Members tune to frequencies, measured in megahertz (MHz), to hear broadcasts and to talk with one another by voice, Morse code, or still pictures. The radio is an internal facility of the station; it does not use real radio equipment.

13.1 The Radio Player and the Radio Console

The radio can be operated in two places.

The radio player is docked on every page except the radio console. It shows the power switch, the frequency, and what is playing. Choose R to expand it and reveal tuning, scanning, volume, MUTE, and FX controls, and OPEN RADIO. Because the player is present on every page, you may listen while you use other services.

The radio console, opened with the RADIO tab or OPEN RADIO, offers the complete set of controls: a frequency display, a band selector, a live signal monitor, the Station broadcast directory, and controls for transmitting.

13.2 Listening

  1. Choose POWER: OFF. The switch changes to POWER: ON.
  2. Tune a frequency in any of these ways:
    • Type a frequency, such as 7.25, in the Frequency MHz field and choose TUNE (or press Enter).
    • Choose TUNE − 0.50 or TUNE + 0.50 to step down or up.
    • Choose a Band to move to it.
    • Choose < SCAN or SCAN > to search for the next active frequency.
    • Choose an entry in the Station broadcast directory.
  3. Adjust Volume, or choose MUTE.

The display shows the name and message of the broadcast, the current title and artist, and the band. FX adds an analogue receiver sound to what you hear; choose it again to hear the plain signal.

NOTE

Browsers will not play sound until you have clicked or tapped on the page. If the radio is on but silent, choose any control on the page.

13.3 Bands

The frequency range is divided into bands, each set aside for a particular purpose and kind of transmission. A new station has the following bands; the SYSOP may change them.

Band Range (MHz) Use
BROADCASTS 7.00–13.99 Hosted programmes
MORSE / CW 14.00–27.99 Morse code
DATA TRANSMISSIONS 28.00–144.99 Still pictures (SSTV)
GENERAL COMMUNICATION 145.00–432.99 Live voice
EXPERIMENTAL / UNRESTRICTED 433.00–999.99 Morse and pictures

Whether you may transmit on a band depends on the station's radio permissions: FULL allows receiving and transmitting, LISTEN allows receiving only, and DENIED allows neither. Only one person may transmit on a frequency at a time.

13.4 Transmitting

When you tune to a band on which you may transmit, the console shows the controls for that band. If the band supports more than one kind of transmission, choose one under Active mode.

Live voice. Hold HOLD TO TALK [V], or hold the V key while you are not typing in a field, and speak. Release to stop transmitting and clear the frequency for others. The browser will ask permission to use the microphone the first time. Voice travels directly between browsers and is never recorded.

NOTE

Browsers permit microphone use only on secure connections. Live voice works at the host computer itself, and from other computers only when the station is reached through an https:// address. The console reports this condition if it applies.

Morse code (CW). Choose BEGIN CW TRANSMISSION, then hold HOLD KEY [SPACE] (or the Space bar) for each dot and dash. Tone Hz sets the pitch and Speed WPM the speed in words per minute. Select CW decoder to have received Morse shown as text. A MORSE REFERENCE table is provided.

Pictures (SSTV). Choose a Local image, check the preview, and choose TRANSMIT PICTURE. Pictures are sent at a small size (80 by 60 pixels) line by line. A picture you receive builds up in the RX PICTURE box; choose SAVE RECEIVED to keep it.

13.5 Hosted Broadcasts

Members whom the SYSOP has authorized to host broadcasts see a MANAGE BROADCASTS button and a Broadcast console on the radio console. 20. The Station MUD

The MUD is a text-based, multiplayer role-playing game. You explore a world described in words, meet other players and the game's characters (called NPCs), take on quests, gather possessions, and fight opponents. A station may run several worlds, and each world is a separate game.

20.1 Choosing a World and Creating a Character

Choose MUD. The Choose a world page lists the worlds the station offers. Each world requires its own character; your progress, skills, possessions, and coins remain in the world where they were earned.

  1. Choose CREATE CHARACTER beside a world.
  2. Work through the steps, choosing NEXT and BACK as required:
    1. Identity: a character name of 2 to 32 letters. Spaces, apostrophes, periods, and hyphens are permitted. Other players see this name, which is separate from your StationNet username.
    2. Race: each race lists its traits and attribute adjustments.
    3. Archetype: your character's calling, with its own bonuses.
    4. Attributes: distribute the points shown among Strength, Agility, Intellect, and Vitality.
    5. Skills: select the number of starting skills shown.
    6. Faction: offered only in worlds that have factions.
    7. Review.
  3. Choose CREATE CHARACTER.

Your choices cannot be changed later except by deleting the character with DELETE CHARACTER and beginning again. To return to a world, choose CONTINUE. To switch worlds, choose CHANGE WORLD.

20.2 The MUD Screen

The GAME panel carries a running account of what happens around you. At its top are the name of your current location and three buttons: ROOM shows the full description of the location with its exits, items, NPCs, enemies, and other players; LATEST returns to the newest activity; and CLEAR empties the account.

Below it is the command line. At the side, panels show your Character (level, health, mana, experience, attributes), Inventory, Quests, and, in some worlds, faction Standing. Choose a panel's title to open or close it.

20.3 Entering Commands

Type in the command line and press Enter or choose SEND.

  • Text without a leading slash is speech, heard by everyone in the same location.
  • Text beginning with / is a command, such as /look.
  • While you type a command, suggestions appear; press Tab to accept one.
  • Press ↑ and ↓ to recall earlier commands.
  • Enter /help for a list of commands, or /help <command> for details of one.

Where a name contains spaces, enclose it in quotation marks: /get "Red Apple" from "Oak Chest".

20.4 Moving About

Each location, or room, has exits leading to other rooms. /exits lists them.

/go north        Leave by the north exit
/north  or  /n   The same, in short form
/look            Describe the room again
/map             Show a map of the surrounding area

The directions north, south, east, west, northeast, northwest, southeast, southwest, up, and down may each be used as a command or abbreviated (/n, /ne, /u, and so on). Some exits have names instead of directions; use /go <exit name>.

In open country, /travel <direction> walks steadily in one direction, one room at a time, until something of interest occurs; /stop halts the journey. /directions <place> gives the way to a place you know, and /destinations lists the places you know.

20.5 Talking to Players and Characters

Command Purpose

/tell "<name>" <text> Speak privately to one player
/reply <text> Answer the last private message
/emote <action> Describe an action
/who List players in the world

20.6 Possessions

/inventory (or /i) lists what you carry and wear. /get and /drop pick up and put down items; /open, /close, and /put work with containers; /equip and /unequip wear or remove gear; /use uses or reads an item. Where a merchant is present, /shop shows what can be bought and sold.

20.7 Quests

NPCs may offer quests. /quests lists your active quests and their objectives, which are also shown in the Quests panel. Many worlds use /quest available, /quest accept, and /quest turnin to find, accept, and complete quests.

20.8 Combat and Recovery

Some NPCs are hostile. /attack <enemy> attacks; /defend reduces the damage you take and protects your companions; /flee attempts escape; /cast <spell> uses a known spell (see /spells); /combat reports the state of the fight. Outside combat, /rest restores part of your health and mana.

If your character is defeated, it recovers at the starting location with half its health. In ordinary worlds, part of the experience gained toward the next level is lost, but levels are never lost.

As your character gains experience it rises in level and earns attribute points, spent with /train, and skill points, used with /learn and /practice. /character shows your full statistics and /skills your skills.

20.9 Dungeons and Expeditions

Some worlds contain dungeons that are generated afresh for each visit. Where the station offers the procedural dungeon world, /expedition starts a random expedition and /daily enters the station-wide Dungeon of the Day. Other players may join an expedition with /join <code>. /run shows its progress, /complete finishes it from the exit, and /abandon leaves at once.

NOTE

The commands available depend on the world. Many worlds add banking, housing, professions, vehicles, and other systems. /help always lists the commands that apply where you are.

20.10 Building Worlds with World Forge

Builders and the SYSOP create and edit worlds with World Forge, linked as WORLD FORGE in the navigation bar and from each world's MUD page. The first screen lists the station's installed worlds and any new worlds not yet installed. Create a world starts a world with one arrival room; Import world ZIP opens a world package as a new draft.

Drafts. Opening a world edits its draft, a copy kept on the station and shared by every builder. Each change is saved to the draft at once; UNDO and REDO step through recent changes, and CHECKPOINT (Ctrl+S) records a backup that Backups & recovery can restore. Players see nothing until the draft is published. Builders editing the same draft are named in the header.

Tabs.

  • World map. The Grid view places rooms on numbered squares, draws passages, and paints wilderness terrain with the brush, rectangle, and ellipse tools. Layers hold upper and lower floors, joined by up and down passages. The Connections view diagrams rooms that have no grid position.
  • Content library. Every kind of content: items, characters, dialogue, quests, factions, shops, professions, housing, vehicles, transit, triggers, dungeon templates, terrain presets, races, classes, skills, abilities, and effects. Selecting a record opens its guided editor; the JSON tab edits it directly. Used by lists what refers to it, and records still in use cannot be deleted.
  • Review. Checks the draft for errors before it is published.
  • Handbook. The creator handbook: every field, with examples.
  • Live world. Acts on the running world: who is where, property holdings, vehicles, script state, and builder access. These actions take effect at once and do not change the draft.

Publishing. PUBLISH checks the draft and shows what will change. A draft that only changes text and numbers is applied in place, and players notice nothing but the new text. Adding, removing, or reconnecting content upgrades the world: characters keep their levels, quests, reputation, and possessions; characters standing in a removed room move to the starting room; items lying in rooms return to their authored places; and dungeon runs in progress end. A new world is installed hidden; the SYSOP enables it under MUD service → Worlds. If a world is upgraded from Settings while a draft is open, the draft is marked out of date and may be rebased from Backups & recovery.

NOTE

The home world may be edited and published like any other. Take a station backup (Section 40.1) before its first full publish, which converts the home world to the package format.

Part VI

Station Hosting and Administration

This part is for the SYSOP. It describes how to install StationNet, start and operate stations, and administer accounts and content.

33. Installation

33.1 The Software, the Station, and Its Data

Three things should be kept distinct:

  • StationNet is the software: the program and its supplied files. It never writes to its own folder.
  • A station is one configured, running service with its own name and port.
  • A station's data is kept in its own folder under stations/. Every account, message, upload, installed program, theme, world, and reference collection belongs to the station folder.

A typical installation is arranged as follows:

StationNet/
  run.py, stationnet/, .venv/   the software (packaged: core/)
  stations/
    default/                    one folder per station
      station.json              name, port, network access, hours
      station.db                the station database
      uploads/, themes/, ...    files, programs, worlds, WIKI data
      run/                      status and log while running

33.2 Requirements

The host computer may run Windows, Linux, macOS, or Raspberry Pi OS. Source installations require Python 3.11 or newer. Packaged Windows builds include everything they need. Packaged Linux and Raspberry Pi builds require Python 3.11 with venv. The Station Manager requires Tkinter (python3-tk on Debian and Raspberry Pi OS). StationNet uses no separate database server.

33.3 Installing a Packaged Release

Windows. Unzip the package and double-click install.bat. The software is installed to %LOCALAPPDATA%\StationNet. Start a station with start.bat in that folder, or manage several with manager.bat. To run without installing, run start.bat from the unzipped folder.

Linux and Raspberry Pi. Unzip the package for your platform and run:

sh install.sh
~/.local/share/stationnet/start.sh

The Station Manager is ~/.local/share/stationnet/manager.sh. Packaged releases install without network access.

Android. The StationNet Android application hosts a station on a 64-bit ARM phone running Android 8 or later. Tap Start station, complete the first-run setup in the phone's browser, then enable the phone's hotspot so that nearby devices can connect to the addresses shown. The Android application does not yet include the WIKI reader.

33.4 Installing from Source

Windows: double-click install.bat, then start with run.bat (one station) or manager.bat (several stations).

Linux and macOS:

chmod +x install.sh run.sh manager.sh uninstall.sh
./install.sh
./run.sh

Source installation downloads Python packages and the WIKI reader.

33.5 First-Run Setup

When a new station starts for the first time, open http://127.0.0.1:8080 on the host computer. The Initialize local station page asks for:

  • The Station name (2 to 80 characters) and Station description.
  • The Administrator username and Password of the first SYSOP.
  • The Registration policy: Open, Private, or Disabled.

Choose INITIALIZE STATION. The station and its SYSOP each receive a callsign, and the SYSOP is signed in.

CAUTION

The account created at first-run setup is the station's only SYSOP account. Record its password safely. StationNet provides no means of creating further SYSOP accounts through the station's pages.

34. Starting, Stopping, and the Station Manager

34.1 Running a Single Station

run.bat, ./run.sh, or a packaged start.bat/start.sh starts the default station in a console window. Press Ctrl+C in that window to stop it. To run another station from the command line, give its identifier:

python run.py --station <id>

A station that is already running cannot be started a second time.

34.2 The Station Manager

The Station Manager (manager.bat or manager.sh) is a desktop window that lists every station under stations/ with its name, status, port, and addresses. Each station card provides:

Start, Stop, Restart
Control the station. Stations started here continue running if the manager window is closed. A station that has not stopped within 20 seconds offers Force Stop.
Open
Opens the station in the browser.
Logs
Opens the station's log.
Settings
Changes the name, port, network access, and the Served through an HTTPS proxy option. Changes to a running station take effect when it restarts; Save & Restart does both.
Hours
Sets opening and closing times (Chapter 36).
Delete
Removes the station permanently, after you type its name to confirm.

To create a station, choose + New Station. The manager suggests a name and a free port and asks who may connect. It can also create the first SYSOP account, so that the browser setup page is not needed.

A station shown as CRASHED stopped unexpectedly; its last log line is displayed. Start it again, and examine the log if the problem recurs.

35. Network Access

By default a station listens on every network adapter of the host, on port 8080. Other devices connect to http://<host-address>:8080. If they cannot, permit incoming TCP connections on the station's port in the host's firewall.

In the Station Manager's Settings you may restrict a station to this computer only, to all adapters, or to one chosen address. For source installations, the environment variables STATIONNET_HOST and STATIONNET_PORT override the station's settings.

35.1 Secure Connections

Browsers allow the microphone (radio voice), application installation, and PSP emulation only on secure connections. To provide them to other computers, place StationNet behind an HTTPS reverse proxy with a certificate trusted by every client. For example, with Caddy:

stationnet.lan {
    tls internal
    reverse_proxy 127.0.0.1:8080
}

Set the station to listen on this computer only, select Served through an HTTPS proxy in the Station Manager (or set STATIONNET_HTTPS=1), make stationnet.lan resolve to the host, and install the proxy's root certificate on each client.

CAUTION

Enable the HTTPS proxy setting only when clients actually use the https:// address. With it enabled, sign-in does not work over plain http://.

Live voice travels directly between browsers and is designed for clients on the same local network.

36. Station Hours, Sleep, and Wake

Station hours are set from the Station Manager's Hours button and take effect at once. They are kept only while a Station Manager window is open (it may be minimized).

  • Closing. For a few minutes before closing (five by default) the card shows a countdown and a Stay Online button, which keeps the station open until its next opening time. Otherwise the station is stopped cleanly at closing time.
  • Opening. A stopped station is started at its opening time. A station you stop by hand during its hours stays stopped until the next opening.
  • Messages. The header line, and the Extended and Early Open messages, may be edited.
  • Sleep. With SLEEP selected, the computer is put to sleep after the station closes, following a Cancel Sleep countdown, unless another station is still running.
  • Wake. The manager sets a wake timer before the earliest next opening. Sleep & Wake in the manager's toolbar sets the countdown and lead time and shows the next scheduled wake.

On Windows, wake timers must be allowed in Power Options → Sleep → Allow wake timers, and some computers using Modern Standby ignore them. On Linux and macOS a wake_command must be configured in manager.json; see the README.

38. Accounts, Permissions, and Moderation

38.1 Delegating Duties

There is one SYSOP account. Duties are delegated to members through groups, which are managed under SYSOP CONTROL → Access & Members:

  • Permission groups. Create a group with CREATE GROUP. Give it HOST BROADCASTS (radio hosting) or MODERATE IMAGEBOARD (pin, lock, and delete on the imageboard), and add radio access rules (FULL, LISTEN, or DENIED for a band, range, or frequency).
  • Group membership. Open a member and select the groups they belong to. Every member also belongs to the permanent DEFAULT group. Across groups, DENIED overrides FULL, and FULL overrides LISTEN.
  • Events. Event creation may be limited to selected groups.
  • MUD building. In World Forge (Live world → Builder access), the SYSOP may authorize members as builders. Builders may edit and publish any authored world; only the SYSOP may change a world's progression or starting room, and new worlds stay hidden until the SYSOP enables them.

38.2 Moderation

The MODERATION tab lists accounts, current restrictions, Open reports, and a searchable Audit history. For each report, read the reported item and choose RESOLVE or DISMISS with a note.

Open an account to record an action. Actions are formal warnings (delivered by private mail), temporary suspensions, permanent bans, chat mutes, and reinstatement. A reason is required; private notes are visible only to SYSOPs. DELETE USER CHAT removes the member's recent public chat messages. The account page also allows an account to be deleted.

A suspended or banned member cannot sign in and is shown the reason. Temporary restrictions expire automatically.

38.3 Passwords

When a member uses FORGOT PASSWORD, the station mails a temporary password to the SYSOP. Confirm the member's identity before passing it on.

39. Managing Modules and Content

Bulletins and announcements. Remove any bulletin, reply, or chat message from its ▾ menu. Post announcements with NEW ANNOUNCEMENT +.

Videos, Library, and Arcade. Each has a Community mode (members upload, within per-member quotas) and a Curated mode (station uploads only). Set the default quota and per-member overrides, and upload station material, in the service's settings card. The Videos module also has a maximum video file size setting for member and station uploads, configurable from 1 to 1,048,576 MB. The Library has categories, an option to allow downloads, and a maximum file size. The Arcade section manages emulator cores: each platform's preferred core can be chosen, and a missing core can be downloaded while the host has Internet access. PSP uploads have their own size limit.

Television. Set the schedule time zone, upload size limit, and default channel, then choose OPEN TV MANAGEMENT. There you create channels (+ NEW CHANNEL), build the Programming library by uploading videos or by copying them into the import folder and choosing SCAN IMPORT FOLDER, and arrange programmes into blocks on the Schedule. Weekly blocks repeat; one-off blocks override them. The Auto scheduler fills a channel's schedule from categorized programmes; review the proposed schedule and choose PUBLISH. Nothing goes on the air until published.

Radio. Set band names, ranges, and permitted transmission types under Band allocations; assign each broadcast host a frequency and set the maximum continuous session under Hosted broadcasting; and set audio storage quotas under Broadcaster storage. MANAGE AUTOMATED BROADCASTS schedules programmes to start at set times or intervals.

WIKI. Place .zim reference files in the station's kiwix_data folder (the path is shown in the WIKI settings card) and turn WIKI on in the module switchboard. New files are found on the next visit. Install only collections from sources you trust.

Programs. Choose PROGRAMS under the management consoles (Manage programs). Upload a program package ZIP and choose VALIDATE + INSTALL. Installed programs can be disabled, re-enabled, or uninstalled. Built-in programs can be disabled but not removed. Uninstalling keeps members' saved data.

MUD. Enable the MUD module, then enable individual worlds under MUD service → Worlds. Worlds are imported from ZIP files; several may run at once. The section also imports races, classes, skills, abilities, and dungeon themes, and offers an example world to download. World Forge (Section 20.10) builds and edits worlds in the browser.

40. Backup, Upgrade, and Maintenance

40.1 Backing Up

To back up a station, stop it and copy its entire folder under stations/. That folder contains the station's identity, accounts, messages, uploads, installed programs, themes, and worlds. Reference collections (.zim files) in kiwix_data are also kept there and may be very large.

The Station Manager's Delete command offers Back up the station first, which saves a ZIP of the folder, without .zim files. To restore a backup, unzip it into stations/.

WARNING

A station folder contains every member's private mail and the station's secret key. Store backups securely and never publish them.

40.2 Upgrading

Back up stations/ first. Stop every station. Then:

  • Windows package: unzip the new release and run its install.bat. The software is replaced; stations is left alone.
  • Linux package: unzip the new release and run its install.sh.
  • Source: replace the source files, keeping stations/, and run the installer again.

Installations older than the stations/ layout are migrated automatically on first start. python run.py --migrate-only --dry-run previews the migration.

40.3 Uninstalling

uninstall.bat or ./uninstall.sh offers to back up station data, then removes the generated environment and station data, leaving the StationNet software ready to be installed again.

40.4 Shutting Down Safely

Always stop a station with Stop in the Station Manager, or Ctrl+C in its console window, before shutting down the host computer or copying the station's folder. The station then closes its database cleanly. Continuous radio broadcasts and similar activities are restarted automatically at the next start.

41. Project Organization

StationNet is a Python application built on Flask, served by Uvicorn, with data in SQLite. Pages are rendered on the server, with JavaScript where needed. No front-end build step is required to run it; the Office programs and some other components are supplied as prebuilt bundles.

Each service is a Flask blueprint. A new service is registered in the same manner and should use the common page template (base.html) and the module switch mechanism in modules.py.

42. Programs and Packages

The most direct way to extend a station is to write a program for the Program Manager. A program is a ZIP file containing manifest.json and a JavaScript entry point (or a BASIC .bas file). It runs in an isolated worker with no access to the page, the network, or the file system. A minimal manifest:

{
  "package_format": 1,
  "slug": "my-application",
  "name": "My Application",
  "description": "A short explanation.",
  "author": "Your name",
  "version": "1.0.0",
  "category": "Utilities",
  "entrypoint": "main.js"
}

and a minimal program:

StationNet.start((ui) => {
  ui.render(ui.title("Hello"), ui.text("Welcome."));
});

The runtime provides interface elements (text, menus, forms, tables, calendars), a canvas with sprites and framebuffers, keyboard, pointer, touch gamepad, and fullscreen support, sound and General MIDI playback, per-user and shared storage, score tables, and multiplayer sessions. Packages are limited to 64 files and 5 MiB compressed. Place manifest.json at the root of the ZIP.

The SYSOP installs packages through Manage programs. The complete reference is stationnet/application_packages/APIREADME.md.

43. Themes

A colour theme is a JSON file defining a site palette, a masthead palette, or both. Start from the template copied to the station's themes/ folder (also stationnet/theme_templates/template-theme.json), give it a unique ID, name, and author, and edit the colours. The SYSOP imports it under Station settings → IMPORT COLOUR THEME. The format is described in stationnet/theme_templates/THEMES_README.md. Built-in palettes are defined in stationnet/themes.py.

44. MUD Worlds and Content

MUD worlds are distributed as ZIP files containing rooms, exits, items, NPCs, dialogue, quests, triggers, wilderness, dungeons, and their own races, classes, and skills. They may be built in World Forge and exported, or written directly as files. The complete format reference is the creator handbook, available from World Forge's Handbook tab. Races and classes, skills, abilities and effects, and dungeon themes may also be distributed separately as JSON. Each kind has a downloadable template in SYSOP CONTROL → MUD.

45. Testing, Building, and Further References

Tests. Install the development requirements and run the test suite:

python -m pip install -r requirements-dev.txt
python -m pytest

Browser-side tests are run with the npm run test:* scripts in package.json.

Rebuilding bundles. After changing an Office program or the MIDI engine, run npm ci, then the matching script: npm run build:documents, build:sheets, build:presentations, build:draw, build:agenda, build:basic, or build:midi.

Distribution. build-all.ps1 builds every package the build computer can produce and places them in dist/, where the GET YOUR OWN STATION page offers them.

Appendix C. Troubleshooting

The station address does not open. The station may be stopped or outside its hours, the address or port may be wrong, or the host's firewall may be blocking connections. Confirm the address with the SYSOP. SYSOPs: check the station's status in the Station Manager and permit the port in the firewall.

"Invalid username or password." Usernames and passwords are case-sensitive. Use FORGOT PASSWORD as described in Section 2.5.

"Account suspended" or "Account banned." A SYSOP has restricted the account. The message shows the reason and any expiry time.

A service described in this manual is missing. The station does not offer it, or it requires you to sign in.

The radio or a video is silent. Click anywhere on the page; browsers block sound until you do. Check MUTE, the volume, and your device's volume.

Live voice reports that the microphone is unavailable. Microphones require a secure connection. Use the station's https:// address, if it has one, or use the host computer itself. Allow microphone access when the browser asks.

"Your chat is muted." A SYSOP has muted your chat. You may still read.

A direct chat has disappeared. Direct chats are erased when either member disconnects. This is normal.

Office will not unlock. The OFFICE Password is different from your StationNet password. If it has been lost, saved Office files cannot be recovered.

The browser offers no "Install" command. Installation requires a secure address. See Chapter 30.

A PSP game will not start. PSP games require a secure connection or the host computer, and WebGL 2.

WIKI reports LIBRARY UNAVAILABLE. SYSOPs: place .zim files in the folder shown in the WIKI settings section.

The Station Manager shows CRASHED. Read the last log line shown, open Logs, and correct the cause, such as a port already in use. Then start the station again.

A station will not start: port in use. Another station or program is using the port. Choose a different port in Settings.
